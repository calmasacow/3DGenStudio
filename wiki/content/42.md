# Game-Ready

Analyse the mesh against the budgets a game engine cares about and get a red/amber/green checklist back: triangle count, topology, UVs, materials, scale and pivot. It's the finishing pass — run it before you export.

Unlike every other mode in the Mesh Editor, the Game-Ready check **never modifies the mesh**. There is no Keep/Revert because there is nothing to revert; it only reports. The one exception is the fix buttons you choose to click (see below).

> The check runs on the **Python mesh-tools service**. Make sure it is configured and running in **Settings → Mesh Tools**. In the desktop app the service is started for you.

## Running it

1. Open the **Game-Ready** mode in the left toolbar.
2. Set the **Budgets** at the bottom of the panel to suit the asset.
3. Click **Run Check**.

A summary banner reports the verdict — *"Game-ready — everything passed"*, *"Ready, with 2 warnings"*, or *"3 blocking issues"* — followed by the findings grouped by area.

The check analyses the mesh **as it would actually ship**, materials and textures included, not just the raw geometry.

## Reading the report

| Icon | Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🟢 | **Pass** | Within budget. |
| 🟠 | **Warning** | Works, but will cost you somewhere — performance, texture quality, or an awkward surprise in the engine. |
| 🔴 | **Fail** | Blocking. Baking, lightmapping, physics cooking or import will go wrong. |
| ⚪ | **Info** | Reported for context, never graded. |

Findings the editor can fix carry a button. Most of them **switch you to the mode that fixes it** (*Fix in Optimize*, *Fix in Auto UV*, *Fix in Auto Retopo*, *Fix in Repair* — Repair has no mode of its own, its controls live inside the Auto Retopo panel). Two of them are single, unambiguous operations with nothing to configure, so they apply straight away: **Set pivot on the ground** and **Centre the pivot**. Both are ordinary undo steps (`Ctrl+Z`), they move the rig along with the mesh, and the check re-runs itself afterwards so you see the finding go green.

## The checks

### Geometry

| Check | Graded on |
| --- | --- |
| **Triangle count** | Warns above the triangle budget, **fails** at double it. → *Optimize* |
| **Vertex count** | Info only. |
| **Vertex normals** | Warns when the mesh has none. |

### Topology

| Check | Graded on |
| --- | --- |
| **Non-manifold edges** | **Fails** on any. Edges shared by more than two faces — baking, boolean operations and physics cooking all fail on these. → *Repair* |
| **Open edges** | Warns on any. The surface is not closed: fine for a plane or a shell, a hole in anything meant to be solid. → *Repair* |
| **Watertight** | Warns when the mesh is not closed. → *Auto Retopo* |
| **Degenerate faces** | Warns on any. Zero-area triangles produce invalid (NaN) normals. → *Repair* |
| **Duplicate faces** | Warns on any. Coincident triangles z-fight and double-shade. → *Repair* |

### UVs

| Check | Graded on |
| --- | --- |
| **UV coordinates** | **Fails** when missing — nothing can be textured, baked or lightmapped without UVs. → *Auto UV* |
| **UV overlap** | The share of covered texels that more than one triangle writes to. Warns above 0.5%, **fails** above 5%. Intentionally mirrored parts show up here too — that is a real finding, because shared UVs are exactly what breaks a bake. Very dense meshes are sampled rather than fully rasterised, marked *(sampled)*. → *Auto UV* |
| **UVs outside 0–1** | Warns on any. Coordinates outside the unit square rely on texture wrapping — intentional for a tiling material, a bug for a baked atlas. → *Auto UV* |
| **Texel density** | Median texels per metre, plus the **spread** (p95 ÷ p5). Warns above ×4, and more strongly above ×8. The spread matters more than the absolute number: a consistent atlas can always be rescaled, an inconsistent one is why one wall of a prop looks crisp and the next looks smeared. → *Auto UV* |

### Materials

| Check | Graded on |
| --- | --- |
| **Materials** | Warns above the *Max materials* budget — each material is a draw call, so past a handful it's worth merging into an atlas. |
| **Textures** | Info only; reports the count and the largest texture's size. |
| **Sub-meshes** | Info only. |

### Transform

| Check | Graded on |
| --- | --- |
| **Size** | The bounding-box extents in metres. Warns above 50 m (the source is probably in centimetres — Unreal expects cm, Unity and glTF expect metres) or below 1 cm. |
| **Pivot** | With *Expect pivot on the ground* **off**, warns when the geometry sits far from its origin, which makes the asset rotate about a point outside itself. → *Centre the pivot*. With it **on**, warns unless the mesh rests on Y=0 centred on X/Z. → *Set pivot on the ground*. |

## Budgets

What "game-ready" means isn't universal — a hero prop and a background rock disagree about every threshold — so the grading budgets are yours to set.

| Field | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Triangle budget** | 50,000 | Warns above this, **fails** at double it. Set it by role: a few thousand for background dressing, tens of thousands for a hero asset. |
| **Texture resolution** | 2048 | The atlas size texel density is measured against. It doesn't change the mesh — it's the assumption behind the px/m figure. |
| **Max materials** | 4 | Each material costs a draw call. |
| **Expect pivot on the ground** | Off | **Turn it on** for props and characters that must snap to the floor when placed in a level; leave it **off** for anything that should rotate about its own centre. |

---

Nothing is modified — this only reports. Fixes are applied by the mode you jump to, or by the two pivot buttons, and every one of them is undoable.
